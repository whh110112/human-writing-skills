# Formal and Official Document Skill

## Aim

Write official notices, memoranda, requests, reports, instructions, minutes, and
administrative documents with clear authority, scope, responsibility, and action.

## Structure

- Identify issuer, audience, purpose, governing basis, and effective scope.
- Put the requested decision, required action, or reported fact before background.
- Separate facts, basis, analysis, decision, responsibility, deadline, and attachment.
- Use headings and lists only when the document convention benefits from them.
- Keep names, titles, document numbers, dates, amounts, citations, and defined terms stable.

## Register Controls

- Use formal, neutral, institutionally appropriate wording.
- Calibrate obligation precisely: distinguish must, shall, should, may, and recommended.
- Use titles and address forms consistently with hierarchy and document convention.
- Remove conversational fillers, chatty particles, performative intimacy, slang, and
  emotional interjections unless they occur inside a preserved quotation or transcript.
- Do not add ceremonial boilerplate that changes no duty, basis, scope, or decision.
- Prefer explicit actors and actions where passive wording would hide responsibility.
- End with the applicable decision, responsible actor, deadline, effective scope,
  remaining limitation, contact, or attachment convention; omit ceremonial uplift that
  changes no duty, status, or decision.

## Avoid

- unsupported legal or policy authority
- vague responsibility, deadline, or scope
- promotional adjectives and emotional persuasion
- novelistic scene-setting or spoken-language texture
- changing quoted wording, figures, terms, or conclusions during stylistic cleanup
