# Attention Budget Audit

## Aim

Check whether the draft spends its words on the material readers were promised.
Detect expansion that increases length without proportional action, evidence,
decision, relationship movement, or interpretive value.

## Activation Gate

Use for long scenes, chapters, expansions, or explicit pacing and dilution reviews.
Keep it out of short drafts and ordinary proofreading.

## Budget Map

1. State the scene or section contract in one sentence.
2. Group paragraphs by function:
   - promise or task
   - attempt and resistance
   - evidence or observation
   - decision and consequence
   - relationship transaction
   - orientation or atmosphere
   - explanation or recap
   - repeated emotional or semantic echo
3. Estimate which functions receive the most space.
4. Compare attention with stakes, novelty, and irreversible change.

## Warning Signs

- The promised process or confrontation receives summary while arrival, weather,
  clothing, or room description receives full treatment.
- Narration, dialogue, and internal thought restate the same fact or emotion.
- A shown action is immediately translated into an emotion label or moral conclusion.
- Expansion adds adjectives, comparisons, and micro-actions but no new constraint,
  choice, evidence, or consequence.
- A low-value transition grows longer than the decision it connects.

Quiet observation is valid when it changes interpretation, establishes usable space,
reveals voice, plants evidence, or increases pressure. Do not require constant plot.

## Repair

Cut or merge semantic echoes first. Move saved attention to the earliest underwritten
decision, resistance, consequence, or relationship turn. Do not solve imbalance by
making every paragraph equally dense.
