# Speech Register and Language Identity

## Aim

Keep each character's spoken language, dialect exposure, politeness, discourse
particles, vocabulary, and code-switching compatible with established identity and
the current audience. Natural speech is evidence-shaped variation, not a quota of
slang, particles, or phonetic accent spelling.

## Activation Gate

Use for fiction or webnovels only when dialogue is present and the task, draft, or
continuity material supplies language, region, upbringing, dialect, honorific,
translation, multilingual, or register evidence. It may also be selected explicitly
as the `register` audit profile. Do not load for narration-only passages, generic
fiction without such evidence, or serious factual prose.

## Language Identity Card

Record only supported dimensions for each important speaker:

```text
primary language and shared scene language
other languages/dialects and demonstrated proficiency
region, upbringing, migration, education, and community exposure
default register and address forms
discourse particles, fillers, contractions, slang, profanity, and idioms
features the character avoids or cannot plausibly use
audience-based shifts and established translation convention
confidence and evidence location
```

Nationality, ethnicity, residence, profession, or one regional label may trigger this
check but does not by itself prove a dialect inventory. A character may speak a common
language without a written accent. Infer no foreign language or regional catchphrase
from identity alone; request or preserve a shared-language decision when it matters.

## Drafting Rules

1. Start from explicit character sheets and repeated prior speech. Use regional or
   cultural priors only as a weak possibility when the text supplies exposure.
2. Keep the scene's shared language legible. If prose is presented in translation,
   do not sprinkle unrelated foreign words merely to signal nationality.
3. Use particles, fillers, contractions, honorifics, slang, and sentence fragments
   where this speaker would use them with this listener. Vary them by intimacy,
   hierarchy, urgency, concealment, fatigue, performance, and emotion.
4. Prefer a few stable lexical, syntactic, address, and response habits over dense
   phonetic spelling. Do not reduce a region or social group to comic orthography.
5. Permit code-switching only through an evidence-backed gate: quotation, learned
   phrase, shared multilingual setting, adaptation to the listener, secrecy, ritual,
   mockery, performance, stress reversion, or explicit translation.
6. Keep vocabulary inside the speaker's knowledge and situation. A useful word must
   fit topic, audience, period, and competence, not merely sound vivid.
7. Preserve intentional spoken ellipsis, but make required meaning recoverable from
   the turn. Particles and hesitations cannot hide an accidentally missing verb,
   object, complement, name, or referent.

## Audit Method

Map representative lines as:

```text
speaker -> listener/audience -> language/register evidence -> observed feature
-> change gate or conflict -> confidence -> minimal repair
```

Flag only evidence-backed problems:

- unsupported switch into another language or dialect
- regional vocabulary incompatible with the established history and no exposure gate
- honorific, title, pronoun, formality, or politeness inconsistent with relationship
- a speaker's stable particles, fillers, profanity, or contractions disappearing or
  spreading to every character without a scene reason
- vocabulary outside the character's knowledge, era, role, or current communicative aim
- every line made colloquial by adding particles, or every line polished into one
  neutral narrator voice
- phonetic accent spelling that harms readability or turns identity into caricature
- apparent colloquialism that is actually a missing grammatical slot

Do not flag a single borrowed word, quotation, joke, translation choice, or deliberate
performance without testing its gate. When evidence conflicts, report uncertainty and
request a ledger decision instead of inventing a linguistic biography.
