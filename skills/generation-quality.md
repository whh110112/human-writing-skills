# Generation Quality Protocol

Apply while writing, not only after an audit. Identify the reader, concrete question
or scene purpose, available material, and evidenced voice before drafting.

- Give each paragraph a job: evidence, explanation, action, response, qualification,
  or consequence. Advance it; do not restate the same point with synonyms.
- Lead with actual information. Cut ceremonial introductions, importance claims,
  inflated transitions, generic advice, and recaps that add nothing.
- Track repeated rhetorical moves, not just words. Keep useful corrections and
  comparisons; avoid automatic threes, slogans, and grand compound labels.
- Let the thought set sentence and paragraph length. Avoid uniform blocks and
  fragment chains without imposing length quotas or banning normal punctuation.
- Details must clarify, support, identify voice, or change interpretation. Avoid
  decorative weather, cups, gestures, and body signals. Direct emotional labels are valid.
- Preserve facts, uncertainty, meaningful roughness, motifs, and speaker identity.
  Never invent memories, quotations, statistics, sources, or personal product tests.
  Narrow a factual answer or identify missing material instead of filling the gap.
- Use target-language information order, idiom, address, and register. Preserve
  evidenced dialect and code-switching; do not import English rhetoric or fake local color.
- Stop when the purpose is fulfilled. Avoid unearned lessons, scenic dissolves,
  promises, and repeated conclusions; keep an informative genre-appropriate ending.

Silently check repetition, unsupported details, voice loss, and closure. Make the
smallest useful repair; keep the original when benefit is unclear. Return prose,
not the checklist or an authorship claim.
