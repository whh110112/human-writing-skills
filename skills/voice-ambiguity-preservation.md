# Voice And Ambiguity Preservation

## Activation Gate

Load only when rewriting or humanizing supplied original text, or when the user
explicitly asks to preserve voice, ambiguity, subtext, motifs, hesitation, or
intentional repetition. Do not load for unrelated new drafting. In audit mode,
require the pre-rewrite original so losses can be shown with evidence.

## Purpose

Prevent cleanup from flattening a specific speaker or resolving pressure the
source deliberately leaves open. Preserve useful irregularity without preserving
accidental confusion.

Before rewriting, make a compact preservation ledger:

| Feature | Source evidence | Function | Rewrite obligation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| uncertainty or double meaning | exact phrase | limits knowledge or sustains subtext | preserve the same uncertainty |
| intentional repetition | repeated word, image, or syntax | pressure, motif, ritual, or idiolect | keep or replace with an equivalent recurrence |
| hesitation or self-correction | pause, restart, fragment | thought or speech under pressure | preserve the search-for-words effect |
| speaker marker | diction, register, taboo, disclosure habit | identifies this speaker | keep frequency and audience fit |
| unresolved interaction | silence, deferral, contradiction | carries debt into the next beat | do not manufacture closure |

## Decision Test

For every rough or ambiguous feature, ask:

1. Is it recoverable from context?
2. Does it reveal voice, limited knowledge, relationship pressure, motif, or theme?
3. Would removing it narrow, resolve, sanitize, or reattribute the meaning?

If yes, protect its function. If no, repair the defect. Distinguish:

- useful ambiguity from unclear reference
- intentional repetition from accidental duplication
- spoken ellipsis from a missing required word
- unstable thought from broken causality
- motif recurrence from decorative image recycling
- calibrated uncertainty from unsupported vagueness

## Genre Boundaries

- Fiction and memoir may preserve fragments, contradiction, silence, self-correction,
  recurring objects, and speaker-specific roughness.
- Essays may preserve a qualified stance, productive tension, and personal cadence.
- News, academic, legal, and technical writing should preserve uncertainty, scope,
  quotation force, and source limits, but not add colloquial roughness for effect.
- Never create fake typos, fake dialect, invented memories, fabricated evidence, or
  arbitrary contradictions as proof of human authorship.

## Audit Output


## Final Check

The rewrite may be clearer, but it must not become more certain, more complete,
more generic, or more socially neutral than the supplied text without permission.
